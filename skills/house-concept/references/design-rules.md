# Design rules of thumb

Starting points for a concept, not a code check. Confirm sizes and setbacks against the local building bye-laws and
the National Building Code that applies, and leave structure to the engineer. Figures below are the commonly quoted
minimums from India's National Building Code 2016 (Part 3) where noted; other countries differ.

## Rooms

| Space | Concept minimum | Comfortable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Habitable room (bedroom, living) | 9.5 m² (≈ 102 sq ft), 2.4 m (≈ 7′-11″) wide (NBC) | 10′ × 12′ to 13′-6″ × 13′-6″ clear |
| Kitchen | 5 m² (≈ 54 sq ft), 1.8 m wide (NBC) | 10′ × 12′, counter on two walls |
| Bath (shower) | 1.8 m², 1.2 m wide (NBC) | 5′ × 7′ |
| WC | 1.1 m², 0.9 m wide (NBC) | 3′-6″ × 6′ |
| Bath + WC combined | 2.8 m², 1.2 m wide (NBC) | 5′ × 8′ |
| Corridor | 1 m (≈ 3′-3″) | 4′ to 6′; widen to a lobby at the middle |
| Clear height, habitable | 2.75 m (≈ 9′) (NBC) | 10′ with a 10′-6″ floor-to-floor |

- Say whether a size is clear (wall face to wall face) or centre-line. Families usually mean clear.
- Walls: 9″ (230 mm) brick outside, 4½″ (115 mm) inside; AAC blocks are lighter and help in high seismic zones.
- Group wet areas back to back so they share a drain stack; keep a WC off the kitchen wall.

## Stairs

| | Main stair | Secondary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type | Dog-leg (two flights + landing) | Spiral, or a straight flight |
| Flight width | 3′ (0.9 m) minimum, 3′-6″ comfortable | Spiral 5′ to 5′-6″ diameter |
| Risers / treads | 7″ (≤ 190 mm) / 10″ (≥ 250 mm): 18 risers for 10′-6″ | Same risers |
| Headroom | 7′-2″ (2.2 m) | One spiral turn ≈ 9′-4″ |
| Plan size | ≈ 7′-6″–8′ × 11′ plus a starting landing | ≈ 30 sq ft |

- A stair inside the house means upper floors are reached through it. If a floor may be let out, give it an outside
  stair or one off an entrance lobby.
- A cabin (mumty) over the stair keeps rain out and carries the water tank.

## Climate

- Hot-humid / heavy monsoon: chajjas (2′) with drip grooves over every window, a 2′–2′-6″ plinth, sloped sills,
  cross ventilation (window → room → corridor or court → opposite room), fanlights over doors, a sloped and
  waterproofed terrace with outlets.
- Courts and light wells: a court open to the sky needs sunk paving, a floor trap and covered galleries; a light well
  over a corridor needs a kerb, grill and a rain cover with open sides.
- Hot-dry: smaller openings on the west, deep shade on the south, a court for night cooling.

## Structure (brief only)

- Design the frame now for the final number of floors: footings, columns, and the roof slab as a future floor.
- Keep the plan regular: columns on a grid, spans of about 10′–15′, no floating columns, no set-backs, no cantilevered
  rooms. Slab openings (stair, light well, court) sit between grid beams; give their edges trimmer bars or edge beams.
- Seismic codes in India: IS 1893 (Part 1) for loads, IS 13920 for ductile detailing, IS 4326 for masonry bands. Look up
  the place's zone; don't guess.
- A soil test comes before footings. A phase added later needs a seismic joint or starter bars left now; the engineer
  decides which.

## Site

- Measure setbacks from the outer wall face to the boundary. Note when a side gap is too small for light, a drain or
  a ladder.
- Put the septic tank where a truck can reach it from the road, and keep any well 15 m (50′) from the soak pit.
- Slope the ground away from the house and run an apron and open drain all round.

## Cost

- Quote cost as a range per sq ft from named, dated sources, split into grey structure and finishes. Show the budget
  divided by the built area next to it. If no local source exists, say so.
- Phasing helps: frame, roof, outer walls and waterproofing first; finishes and phase-2 rooms later.
